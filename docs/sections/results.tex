\section{Results \& Interpretation}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Headline.} Across $\sim$140 seed-replicated runs, hyperbolic
geometry reliably wins on \emph{local} hierarchy structure
(sibling/cousin retrieval) and reliably loses on \emph{global}
classification and tree-Spearman. Adding a hierarchy-aware loss
($\lambda > 0$) helps both geometries on global tree metrics but does
not let hyperbolic surpass Euclidean on classification accuracy. The
choice of geometry should therefore be driven by which type of
hierarchy preservation matters for the downstream application, not by
treating one as universally superior.

\subsection{Phase 1 --- Scaling sweep}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/phase1_scaling.pdf}
  \caption{Phase~1: metrics versus embedding dimension $d$ (90 runs;
  3 seeds per config). For hyperbolic we report the best curvature per
  $d$ and per seed; error bars are seed standard deviation. Top-1
  accuracy saturates near $d=16$ for both geometries, with Euclidean
  ahead by 4--6\,pp at $d \geq 4$. Sibling recall@5 \emph{decreases}
  with $d$ for Euclidean but remains stable for hyperbolic, opening a
  5\,pp gap by $d=64$.}
  \label{fig:phase1}
\end{figure}

Phase~1 sweeps geometry $\times$ $d \in \{2,4,8,16,32,64\}$ $\times$
$c \in \{0.1, 0.3, 1, 3\}$ over three seeds (90 configurations,
$\sim$14~min wall time on a single Apple M-series GPU).
Figure~\ref{fig:phase1} shows three findings. \textit{(i)} Top-1
accuracy saturates near $d=16$ for both geometries: Euclidean reaches
65.9\,\% and hyperbolic 60.6\,\%, with the gap stable rather than
closing as $d$ grows. \textit{(ii)} Local hierarchy structure tells
the opposite story --- sibling recall@5 \emph{decreases} for Euclidean
as $d$ grows (from 0.221 at $d{=}2$ to 0.142 at $d{=}64$) but stays
near 0.19 for hyperbolic. The two geometries are tied on local
hierarchy at $d=2$ and split apart as Euclidean gains capacity.
\textit{(iii)} Curvature trades top-1 for hierarchy fidelity within
hyperbolic alone: at $d=8$, $c=0.3$ maximises top-1 (59.8\,\%) while
$c=3.0$ maximises sibling recall (0.230). Pure scaling does not close
the global-metric gap.

\subsection{Phase 2 --- Hierarchy-aware loss}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/phase2_lambda.pdf}
  \caption{Phase~2: top-1, class-center / tree Spearman, mean tree
  distortion, and sibling recall@5 versus the hierarchy-aware loss
  weight $\lambda$ at $d{=}8$, $c{=}1$ (3 seeds per point). The
  regularizer drives tree-Spearman from $\sim$0.3 to $\sim$0.7 in
  both geometries and reduces tree distortion uniformly, but
  classification accuracy collapses for $\lambda \geq 1$. The
  hyperbolic top-1 advantage \emph{never} catches up to Euclidean.
  Sibling recall is stable for hyperbolic across all $\lambda$; for
  Euclidean it only rises at the largest tested $\lambda$.}
  \label{fig:phase2}
\end{figure}

Phase~2 sweeps $\lambda \in \{0, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3\}$ at $d \in \{8, 16\}$
with $c=1$ and three seeds. The loss is the scale-invariant MSE
between prototype-prototype distances and tree distances introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:methods}. Figure~\ref{fig:phase2} shows the four
panels. \textit{Mild regularization is a Pareto improvement.} At
$\lambda{=}0.1$, Euclidean $d{=}8$ \emph{gains} 0.7\,pp top-1
(65.0\,\% vs.\ 64.3\,\% baseline) and improves tree-Spearman from
0.292 to 0.345; hyperbolic similarly gains slightly on top-1 and gains
2 points of sibling recall. \textit{Strong regularization buys
hierarchy at classification cost.} At $\lambda{=}3$, both geometries
reach tree-Spearman $\approx 0.7$ but top-1 collapses --- to
46.9\,\% for Euclidean and 37.4\,\% for hyperbolic. \textit{The
geometry winner does not flip.} At every $\lambda$ tested, Euclidean
has higher top-1 than hyperbolic, and at every $\lambda$ hyperbolic
has higher sibling recall. The local-vs-global trade-off observed in
Phase~1 is preserved under the regularizer.

\subsection{Phase 3 --- Tree-variant ablation}
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/phase3_tree_variants.pdf}
  \caption{Phase~3: each geometry trained with $\lambda{=}1$ at $d{=}8$
  against three different ground-truth trees, then evaluated against
  the default tree. Bars are seed means; whiskers are seed standard
  deviation. Top-1 stays higher for chronological and flat training
  because their constraint is easier to satisfy without distorting the
  classification objective; tree-Spearman against the default tree
  collapses for chronological and flat (the training signal does not
  align with the eval reference). The geometry winner does not change
  on any panel: Euclidean wins top-1 on every tree, hyperbolic wins
  sibling recall on every tree.}
  \label{fig:phase3}
\end{figure}

Phase~3 fixes $\lambda{=}1$, $d{=}8$, $c{=}1$ and varies the
\emph{training} tree across $\{\textrm{default}, \textrm{chronological},
\textrm{flat}\}$ while always evaluating against the default tree
(otherwise tree-Spearman is not comparable across runs).
Figure~\ref{fig:phase3} shows the result. Training against the
chronological or flat tree pushes top-1 back up to the
$\lambda{=}0$ baseline (around 65\,\% for Euclidean, 60\,\% for
hyperbolic) because the constraint is easier to satisfy ---
chronological is a coarser tree, flat has constant pairwise distances
so the loss can be satisfied trivially. Tree-Spearman against the
default tree drops to $\approx 0.14$ in both alternatives,
confirming that the regularizer only helps the metric anchored to the
\emph{training} tree. Crucially, the local-vs-global trade-off
\emph{does not} depend on the choice of training tree: Euclidean wins
top-1 on every tree (Phase~3 column, Figure~\ref{fig:phase3} panel~1),
and hyperbolic wins sibling recall on every tree (panel~4). The
qualitative conclusion is robust to the specific ground-truth
hierarchy.
